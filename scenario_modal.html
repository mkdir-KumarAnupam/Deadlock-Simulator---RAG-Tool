DEADLOCK
01

Simple Deadlock

Two processes and two resources in a classic mutual hold-and-wait situation. Both processes hold one resource and request the other.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Understand the four necessary conditions for deadlock: mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, and circular wait.
2 Processes 2 Resources
DEADLOCK
02

Circular Wait (3 Processes)

Three processes form a circular chain where each holds one resource and waits for another, creating a cycle in the resource allocation graph.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Visualize how circular wait condition manifests in RAG. Learn cycle detection algorithms and deadlock prevention strategies.
3 Processes 3 Resources
SAFE STATE
03

Safe State Example

Linear resource dependency chain without cycles. Demonstrates a safe allocation where processes can complete sequentially without deadlock.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Distinguish between safe and unsafe states. Understand how proper resource ordering prevents deadlock formation.
3 Processes 2 Resources
STARVATION
04

Resource Starvation

One process monopolizes a resource with long burst time while five other processes compete endlessly for access, demonstrating indefinite postponement.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Understand starvation vs deadlock. Learn aging techniques and fair scheduling to prevent indefinite blocking.
6 Processes 1 Resource
SCHEDULING
05

FCFS Scheduling Test

First-Come-First-Served: Four processes with varying burst times executed in arrival order. Non-preemptive, simple but can cause convoy effect.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Analyze average waiting time in FCFS. Identify convoy effect where short processes wait behind long ones.
Algorithm: FCFS 4 Processes
SCHEDULING
06

SJF Scheduling Test

Shortest Job First: Processes with intentionally varied burst times to demonstrate optimal average waiting time when executed shortest-first.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Prove SJF minimizes average waiting time. Understand the challenge of predicting burst times in real systems.
Algorithm: SJF 4 Processes
SCHEDULING
07

SRTF Preemptive Test

Shortest Remaining Time First: Long-running process repeatedly preempted by incoming short processes. Shows context switching overhead.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Compare preemptive vs non-preemptive scheduling. Observe context switching costs and response time improvements.
Algorithm: SRTF 4 Processes
SCHEDULING
08

Round Robin Test

Time-sharing with quantum=30ms. Four processes with similar burst times take turns executing, providing fair CPU time distribution.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Understand time quantum trade-offs: too small increases overhead, too large reduces responsiveness. Balance fairness and efficiency.
Algorithm: RR (Q=30) 4 Processes
CLASSIC PROBLEM
09

Dining Philosophers

Five philosophers alternately think and eat. Each needs two chopsticks (shared resources) to eat, but only five chopsticks exist.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Study classic synchronization problem. Learn solutions: resource hierarchy, arbitrator, or asymmetric approach.
5 Philosophers 5 Chopsticks
CLASSIC PROBLEM
10

Producer-Consumer

Producers generate data and consumers process it. Shared buffer with limited capacity requires synchronization to prevent overflow/underflow.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Master bounded-buffer problem. Understand semaphores, mutexes, and condition variables for synchronization.
2 Producers 2 Consumers
CLASSIC PROBLEM
11

Readers-Writers

Multiple readers can access shared data simultaneously, but writers need exclusive access. Demonstrates reader/writer priority trade-offs.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Explore reader-writer locks. Compare reader-preference vs writer-preference policies and their starvation implications.
4 Readers 2 Writers
PRIORITY
12

Priority Inversion

High-priority process blocked by low-priority process holding a resource. Medium-priority process runs instead, inverting priorities.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Identify priority inversion problem. Learn priority inheritance and priority ceiling protocols as solutions.
3 Processes Different Priorities
PERFORMANCE
13

Convoy Effect Demo

One CPU-bound process with very long burst time followed by many I/O-bound processes. All short processes wait, reducing system throughput.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Observe convoy effect in FCFS. Understand why preemptive schedulers perform better for mixed workloads.
1 CPU-bound 6 I/O-bound
RECOVERY
14

Resource Preemption

Deadlock occurs and must be resolved by preempting resources from victim processes. Demonstrates one recovery strategy.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
Learn deadlock recovery techniques: victim selection, rollback, and starvation prevention during recovery.
3 Processes Preemption Demo

Multiple Resource Instances

Resources with multiple identical instances. Processes request and release instances dynamically, testing allocation strategies.

Aging Mechanism

Low-priority processes gradually increase their priority over time to prevent indefinite postponement in priority scheduling.

🎯 Learning Outcome:
